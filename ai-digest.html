原文完整摘要

Long-horizon, real-time controllable motion generation is essential for interactive animation and humanoid robotics, where an agent must respond to changing instructions and spatial goals while moving. Existing autoregressive diffusion methods generate motion incrementally, but bounded attention caches limit access to distant history, while repeated denoising introduces substantial inference overhead. We present Motion Mamba 2.0, a Mamba-based root-body diffusion architecture that integrates local attention, resumable recurrent states, and persistent content memory (PCM) for long-horizon controllable motion generation. PCM transforms compressed motion history from Mamba recurrent states, together with state changes and text-conditioned features, into dynamically updated content slots accessible to local attention. To enable efficient inference, we further develop an execution scheme tailored to this architecture, combining CUDA Graph replay, fused kernels, and execution-flow simplification while preserving the canonical history update. Evaluations on retargeted HiPHI demonstrate accurate terminal control over rollouts of up to 39.52 s, while short-horizon comparisons on BONES Seed and retargeted HumanML3D show that the model also retains strong spatial control and text-motion matching. On an RTX 4090, the optimized execution reduces median complete-window latency from 1685.35 to 276.14 ms, achieving a median paired speedup of 6.138$\times$ while preserving identical outputs and committed states in the tested rollouts. Moreover, we conduct extensive real-world evaluation on the Unitree G1, demonstrating sequential text-guided action composition, expressive motion, and interactive locomotion through keyboard and mouse control.

Frontier multimodal models exhibit extraordinary generalization in generating robot actions from observations and instructions, enabling a new paradigm in which they serve directly as policies within closed-loop robotic harnesses. 原文完整摘要

Current leading video generation models can synthesize highly realistic videos, while still frequently violating physical and logical constraints in multi-step reasoning and planning, even after training with large-scale reasoning data or existing diffusion RL methods. We attribute these failures to the conventional video training paradigm, which treats a solution as a fixed-length clip rather than a chain of decisions. We present ViDecide, a decision-centric framework that trains video diffusion models to predict state transitions causally and decide when to stop, with a decision-focused training schedule. AI 摘要节选数据驱动的天气预报需要区分当地状态及其时间演变。然而，学习的模式仍然隐含在神经预测器中，使得它们的影响难以解释。无法区分局部演化可能会导致对发展不同的位置进行类似的预测，从而增加预测误差。这激发了区分和解释本地模式的明确参考。原型提供了明确的模式参考，但样本或任务级调节留下了如何适应不同位置和变量组的余地。为了解决这个问题，我们设计了 ProtoCast，它使用特定于组的原型库来适应局部特征，同时考虑到跨变量依赖性。具体来说，（1）我们将每个组的历史特征和时间变化与来自跨变量注意的预测上下文相结合，以进行进化感知原型匹配。 (2) 特定于原型的低阶算子的稀疏混合针对每个组和位置定制校正。然后，门控注意力块在预测之前跨组交换校正后的特征。在融合之前，每个组的修正都会分解为明确的原型贡献。 （3）我们从头开始联合训练预测网络和算子，并在每个训练周期之前重新调整每个组的原型。

原文完整摘要

Data-driven weather forecasting requires distinguishing local states and their temporal evolution. However, learned patterns remain implicit in neural forecasters, making their influence difficult to interpret. Failing to distinguish local evolution can lead to similar forecasts for locations that develop differently, increasing prediction error. This motivates explicit references for distinguishing and interpreting local patterns. Prototypes provide explicit pattern references, but sample- or task-level conditioning leaves open how to adapt to distinct locations and variable groups. To deal with this problem, we design ProtoCast, which uses group-specific prototype banks to adapt local features while accounting for cross-variable dependencies. Specifically, (1) we combine each group's historical features and temporal change with forecast context from cross-variable attention for evolution-aware prototype matching. (2) Sparse mixtures of prototype-specific low-rank operators tailor corrections to each group and location. A gated attention block then exchanges the corrected features across groups before prediction. Each group's correction decomposes into explicit prototype contributions before this fusion. (3) We jointly train the forecasting network and operators from scratch and refit each group's prototypes before every training epoch. Slot tracking preserves prototype-operator associations, making each prototype's contribution to local corrections more interpretable. Experiments on GlobalForecast and TyphoonTracking show improved forecasting accuracy, while prototype deletion links local feature contributions to changes in prediction error. This work advances the development of more accurate and interpretable data-driven weather forecasting models. Project page: https://zhixuanli.github.io/projects/li2026protocast
