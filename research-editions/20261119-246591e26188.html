Frontier Vision-Language Models (VLMs) such as GPT-6 Astra have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in robot control, suggesting that broad pretraining provides knowledge valuable for physical action. Yet most Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies still rely on much smaller VLM backbones, leaving this potential largely untapped. 原文完整摘要

Semantic completeness alone cannot determine when an assistant should respond in a multi-speaker environment: a completed utterance may belong to an interfering speaker, while the designated user is still speaking, acknowledging, or requesting time. We therefore study semantic turn inference conditioned on speaker identity. We propose SpeakerTurn, a framework that jointly predicts the target transcript and turn state from a speech mixture and an independent target-speaker reference. Built on CocktailASR-1, it links semantic decisions to the corresponding target content through joint supervision, distinguishing complete, incomplete, backchannel, wait, and target-absent states, with a separate interruption task. We also examine whether structured chain-of-thought (CoT) supervision improves decisions by representing speaker presence and semantic cues. On a synthetic multi-speaker test set, SpeakerTurn achieves five-state macro F1 of 0.811 and target-transcription character error rate (CER) of 4.20%, correctly resolving all ten reference-swapped interruption pairs. Joint-format transcription and native-interface recognition nevertheless behave differently, and CoT improves validation transcription without improving turn macro F1. These results show why target attribution, semantic turn discrimination, and recognition retention require separate assessment when adapting speech language models for speaker-conditioned interaction.

Continuous autoregressive TTS relies on an acoustic target that enables stable next-latent modeling while preserving fine-grained acoustic details. While existing approaches employ learned compressed representations (e.g., continuous VAE latents) to render prediction tractable, they rely on complex neural encoders that often cap generation fidelity. Conversely, training-free targets like mel-spectrograms eliminate encoder overhead, yet their potential for autoregressive generation remains largely underexplored. In this work, we propose VoxMel, a tokenizer-free framework for autoregressive TTS that makes stable mel modeling tractable through a fixed, exactly invertible two-axis Haar transform. The transform first decomposes each mel spectrogram along frequency into local averages and a frequency residual, then splits the averaged component along time into Coarse-Mel and a temporal residual. A language-model backbone with a flow-matching head autoregressively generates Coarse-Mel. Non-autoregressive residual tiers then predict the temporal residual from Coarse-Mel, followed by the frequency residual conditioned on the reconstructed Frequency-Averaged Mel. Sources that are not quantization move between 0 and 0.92% of decisions. llama.cpp restarts are bit-identical on every record for both models. vLLM restarts flip 0, 5 and 20 decisions at BF16, FP8 and NVFP4 on Clef-flash and 4, 0 and 22 on Clef; a third instance of Clef's quantized builds (exploratory) leaves FP8 at 0 and puts NVFP4 at 4 to 22, depending on which two instances are compared. An engine change at BF16 flips 6 to 16, a batch-size change 8 to 15, and the Gated DeltaNet kernel transformers selects unasked 10 to 14. Quantization inside one engine moves more: for Clef-flash and Clef, 0.88% and 1.04% at Q8_0, 2.12% and 1.62% at FP8, 5.79% and 3.50% at Q4_K_M, and 5.79% and 5.67% at NVFP4. On both models, every 4-bit interval excludes every non-quantization source. Two of our five pre-registered predictions failed, and the same two fail when the tests are rerun on the 27B: FP8 is not inside restart noise, and changing engine moves fewer decisions than 8-bit quantization does. Accuracy hides most of this. Over the full 31,275 public questions, Clef's Q4_K_M build moves accuracy by a net six questions (-0.02 points [-0.20, +0.16]) while 436 of its decisions get worse (exploratory), and NVFP4 costs Clef-flash 0.95 points and Clef 0.65. We release the code, quantization recipes, per-record outputs and the pre-registration.

原文完整摘要

Robot policies trained by imitation generalize poorly outside the environments and objects seen in training. Published data-scaling studies find that generalization grows with the diversity of training environments and objects rather than with more demonstrations per environment. Robot data is costly to collect across many environments, whereas egocentric human video can be recorded in the wild at scale; existing egocentric video, however, mostly shows daily-life activity and contains little skilled work in real workplaces. We present HubEgo-100K, a dataset built for diversity. This version of the paper covers the first release, v1.0: 10,124 hours of 1080p egocentric video with synchronized 100 Hz inertial (IMU) data from one head-mounted phone camera. In v1.0, 12,227 contributors record their own unscripted work in 15,548 environments (each one contributor's own setting of one type, such as their kitchen or workshop) across 11 setting types, 30 work categories, 56 task types and 35 action families, with 83% of hours in workplace settings. The release comprises 72,754 episodes with machine-generated action and language annotations, in LeRobot and MCAP formats under CC BY 4.0. We measure diversity along each axis, show that inside v1.0, with episodes in random order, new contributors and words keep appearing, and relate the measurements to published evidence on six data properties tied to policy performance, including hand visibility and inertial sensing. We also document collection, curation, privacy measures and data format, and provide a datasheet. Later versions of this paper will add benchmarks and cover further releases toward 100,000 hours.
