原文完整摘要

Reliable robotic manipulation requires both semantic understanding and precise control in three-dimensional space. However, vision-language-action (VLA) foundations largely inherit visual representations from 2D vision-language pre-training, while spatial supervision is often introduced only during task-specific adaptation. Developing spatial understanding as a transferable foundation-model capability therefore calls for learning from broader robot experience. We present SLA, a Spatial-Language-Action Model that scales implicit spatial alignment to continual pre-training and maintains it during downstream post-training. Initialized from a pretrained pi05 policy, SLA jointly optimizes action prediction and alignment between intermediate visual embeddings and spatial representations from a frozen 3D perception model. We curate and unify heterogeneous robot datasets to support spatial learning across diverse embodiments, tasks, and scenes. To accommodate this diversity, we introduce feature-dependent gates that adaptively aggregate visual embeddings from multiple VLA layers for spatial supervision. The teacher further exploits partially available depth and camera calibration alongside RGB observations, enabling effective use of heterogeneous geometric annotations. Probabilistic state dropout encourages reliance on visual cues and mitigates overfitting to demonstration trajectories. All auxiliary components are used only during training, allowing direct reuse of pretrained VLA weights and preserving the original inference architecture.

原文完整摘要

Omni models answer questions about videos by jointly processing sampled frames and audio in one turn, with progress driven largely by scaling model size and omni training data. We ask whether a model needs to hear the audio directly at all, and present SleuthVLM, an agent harness built on a VLM that never hears the audio directly: speech will be transcribed into text, and the VLM will sleuth the video on its own without any predefined workflow. On four omni benchmarks with videos up to 90 minutes, SleuthVLM achieves the best average accuracy: \textbf{75.0%}, 2.6 points above Gemini 3.8 Flash, and it also tops the Video-MME-v2 leaderboard with \textbf{77.8%}; with an open-source 27B VLM, it beats all open-source omni models. We draw three findings: SleuthVLM beats a model that glances; hearing is not the bottleneck, agentic ability is; omni understanding needs better agentic ability, not just more omni data for modality alignment. So we provide a simple but effective starting point for doing omni video understanding. Omni understanding needs better agents, not just bigger models that hear.

原文完整摘要

Active speaker detection (ASD) relies on audiovisual correspondence between speech and visible faces, yet this correspondence becomes unreliable in crowded scenes, or when visual evidence is unavailable. We introduce ROAM-ASD-C and ROAM-ASD-CR to extend ROAM-ASD with contextual and identity-aware reasoning. ROAM-ASD-C jointly reasons across simultaneously visible candidates, allowing other faces to provide complementary evidence when the target observation is ambiguous or incomplete. ROAM-ASD-CR incorporates registered-speaker representations derived from enrollment speech, allowing detection of whether a speaker is active even when that speaker is not visually observed. ROAM-ASD-C achieves state-of-the-art performance across five ASD benchmarks: 96.8% mAP on AVA, 98.9% on WASD, 88.8% on UniTalk, 98.7% on Talkies, and 99.5% on ASW. 原文完整摘要

Diarization-conditioned target-speaker ASR (TS-ASR), in particular Diarization-conditioned Whisper (DiCoW), is an accurate and data-efficient approach to multi-talker transcription. It has, however, three structural limitations: the model transcribes each speaker independently, operates on short (30 s) windows, and optimizes its diarization and TS-ASR components separately. We address the first two. We integrate the diarization conditioning into a longform Parakeet backbone (DiCoP) and add cross-speaker attention, which lets the model attend across the speaker dimension so that speakers are no longer decoded in isolation, and an STNO mask refiner that re-estimates the diarization-derived conditioning. We open-source the resulting models, DiCoP and Pandemonium, and the pseudo-labeled data.
